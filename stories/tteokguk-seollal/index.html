Open Korea 101 in Sori

The New Year arrives in a bowl.

Why tteokguk belongs at the Seollal table.

A holiday can announce itself through a sound, a journey or a smell from the kitchen. During Seollal, Korea’s Lunar New Year, one familiar sign is a bowl of tteokguk: soup with sliced rice cakes.

Eating it on New Year’s morning carries a traditional connection with becoming a year older. The expression is part of the holiday’s story. A bowl of soup does not change your official birthday or age.

Rice cake soup is only one part of the table. Depending on the household, you may also see pancakes, dumplings or other dishes prepared for sharing. There is no single meal that represents every family.

For a visitor, the useful lesson is to leave a little room in the plan. A holiday can change opening hours and the rhythm of a neighbourhood. Check the places you want to visit rather than assuming an ordinary weekday schedule.

You do not need to know every tradition to enjoy learning about it. Start with a bowl, a question and the stories people choose to tell you.
